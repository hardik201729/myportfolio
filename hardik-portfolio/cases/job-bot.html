A job search is a funnel too. So I built one.

Applying through job portals felt like sending CVs into a void. I treated my own job search like a product funnel and built a Python bot that finds roles, scores fit, finds the right people and follows up politely, so I could spend my time preparing for interviews instead of copy-pasting emails.

The short version

Volume wasn't my bottleneck; getting a human to read my application was. The bot pulls jobs from three sources, scores each one on four signals, finds two recruiters and referral contacts per role on a fixed credit budget, sends a tailored email with the right resume, and follows up twice, stopping the moment someone replies.

01

Context

Like most job seekers, I started by applying on portals. After 60 applications I had 1 reply. The applications weren't bad. They just never reached a person.

As a PM, I'd never accept a funnel with a 2% conversion and no visibility into why. So I stopped treating the search as a chore and started treating it as a product.

02

The problem behind the problem

I broke my own search into steps and timed each one. Finding and filtering roles took 40% of my time, finding the right person 30%, writing emails 20% and remembering to follow up the rest. None of it needed my judgement except deciding which roles were worth it.

Automate the repetition. Keep the judgement.
03

How it works

Scoring fit

Every job gets a score from 0 to 100, from four signals I weighted by how well they predicted a reply.

Product decisions in a side project

  • A credit budget, not unlimited lookups. Contact data costs API credits, so the bot spends them only on the best-scoring roles, with a cap per run. Same trade-off as any paid acquisition channel.
  • Politeness as a hard rule. Before every follow-up, the bot checks Gmail for a reply in the same thread. If anyone answers, it stops. Nobody gets a third email.
  • The right resume for the role. Full-stack roles get a different resume from PM roles, chosen automatically from the job title.
  • A dashboard, not a spreadsheet. A local page shows the funnel from jobs found to replies, so I can see which step is leaking.
