Two leaks in one funnel, and why the second one mattered more

Sales were flat and the plan was another discount. An audit of the whole funnel found two leaks instead: an OTP screen losing a third of new users, and a checkout failing for nearly a third of buyers. Fixing both grew signups 18% and lifted subscription revenue by an amount that stays under NDA.

The short version

Demand was not the problem. The funnel was. I mapped install → signup → trial → paid, found the two biggest drop-offs, and fixed them in order of revenue impact: onboarding first (signups +18%), then payments (success → ). No discount needed.

01

Context

PrepLadder helps doctors prepare for NEET PG and FMGE, two exams that decide their specialisation. It has 200K+ active learners and a paying base I can't print here. I was the only PM on the app.

Exam season is when people buy. In the season before I joined, sales had flattened, and the default playbook was a bigger discount. Discounts move the number for a week and train users to wait for the next one.

02

The problem behind the problem

Before agreeing to a discount, I wanted to know where people were dropping out. I combined event data with 40 session replays and 12 calls with users who had installed but never paid.

People weren't saying no to the price. Most never got far enough to see it.

Two leaks stood out. 34% of new users dropped at OTP verification. And of everyone who tried to pay, failed and didn't come back.

03

Fix 1: the first two minutes

Replays showed the same story again and again. OTPs arriving late on busy networks, users switching apps to find the code and not returning, then a seven-question profile before seeing a single lecture.

  • One-tap login with Google and Truecaller, with OTP as the fallback rather than the only door.
  • Deferred profiling. We asked only for the exam on day one. Everything else came after the first lecture.
  • Exam-first Home. A new user landed on content for their exam and attempt year, not a generic catalogue.

We ran it as a 50/50 A/B test for 2 weeks. Signups rose 18%, OTP drop-off fell from 34% to 19%, and day-1 retention went from 41% to 47%.

04

Fix 2: the checkout nobody looked at

Payments sat between product and finance, so nobody owned the failure rate. I pulled 90 days of gateway logs and grouped every failed attempt by cause.

What I learned

  • Audit the whole funnel before touching any step. The most visible problem (signups) wasn't the most expensive one (payments).
  • Find the orphan metric. Payment success sat between two teams, so nobody watched it. Owning it was the biggest win.
  • Ask for the replay, not the report. Dashboards showed where people left; replays showed why.
